About this estimate

The map shows the radiant flux density (irradiance) in a plane through the fiber axis for light leaving a flat-cleaved fiber that touches the tissue. It is computed with the beam-spread-function (BSF) model of McLean et al. (1998), as adapted for optogenetics by Yona et al. (2016): the response to a thin pencil beam, made of unscattered light plus a time-dispersed scattered part, is spread over the fiber's emission cone and over the area of the fiber core.

Tissue optics

  • Reduced scattering is estimated from the wavelength as µs′ = 2.37·(λ/500 nm)−1.15 mm⁻¹ (Liu et al. 2015), with anisotropy g = 0.8803, so µs = µs′/(1 − g).
  • Absorption comes from the blood: hemoglobin spectra compiled by S. Prahl (omlc.org) at the given O₂ saturation and hemoglobin concentration, scaled by the blood volume. Water absorption is negligible below 800 nm and is ignored.
  • The striatum and olfactory-bulb presets are the lowest and highest blood volumes reported in mouse brain (Chugh et al. 2009), as used in Mahn, Klavir & Yizhar (2020), Fig. 11.3.

Limitations

  • This is an analytical approximation for strongly forward-scattering tissue. It treats the emission cone paraxially and ignores light reflected back towards the fiber, so values far from the axis and close to the tip are rough.
  • Real tissue is not homogeneous; blood vessels and fiber tracts change the light distribution. Use the numbers to plan experiments, not as measurements.
  • Opsins respond to light from all directions (fluence rate), which in scattering tissue is higher than the irradiance shown here.
